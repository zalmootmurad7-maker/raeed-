# Episode 8 — What If We Replaced the Moon With Every Planet?  (script v1, Laura-style list format)
Format copied from @lauralonglegs1 ("Every Type Of Star" 57.7k): 3 extreme items in the hook → "let's put every one
where X is and see what happens to YOU" → one section per item, smallest → biggest → every section ends with a teaser
for the next → subscribe line in the middle → recap + comment question. Our identity: impossible scenario + real physics,
hand-drawn, "you" in the story. Target ≈ 7–8 min (ElevenLabs v3, Mark, Speed 1.1; record in 3 files: 1 = hook…Mars,
2 = Venus…Uranus, 3 = Saturn…end).

## Voice script
```
[excited] Swap the Moon for one planet, and you could see a canyon on it longer than the Moon is wide. Swap it for another, and the ocean would rise and fall thirty meters, twice a day. And swap it for the biggest one... and Earth isn't the planet anymore. Earth is the moon.
[mischievously] So let's take every planet, put it exactly where the Moon is, and see what happens to you. From the smallest... to the one that steals our planet.

[curious] Mercury. The smallest planet. It's only about a third wider than our Moon, and it looks a lot like it. Grey, cratered, no air.
Put it where the Moon is, and at first, you might not even notice. It's a slightly bigger, slightly brighter Moon.
But Mercury is mostly iron. It's four and a half times heavier than the Moon. So its pull on the oceans is four and a half times stronger. Every beach you know would sit underwater at high tide.
Mercury barely changes the sky. The next planet changes it a lot, and you'd see its scars with your own eyes.

Mars. Twice as wide as the Moon, and rusty red. Put it in the Moon's place and you'd have a red Moon, every night.
But here's the strange part. You could see its geography without a telescope. Olympus Mons, the biggest volcano in the solar system, would be a dark dot you could spot with your eyes. And Valles Marineris, a canyon four thousand kilometers long, would stretch across Mars wider than the whole Moon looks today.
Its pull on the oceans? About nine times stronger than the Moon's. Coastal cities would flood with every tide.
Mars is about the size you'd expect. The next planet is almost Earth's twin, and it would turn your nights into day.

Venus. Almost as big as Earth, three and a half times wider than the Moon, and wrapped in bright white clouds that reflect most of the sunlight that hits them.
Put it in the Moon's place, and a full Venus would shine about seventy times brighter than a full Moon. You could read a book at midnight.
But the real problem isn't the light. It's the weight. Venus is sixty-six times heavier than the Moon. Today, the tide in the open ocean rises about half a meter. With Venus up there, it would rise around thirty. Twice a day. Every port, every beach, every coastal city on Earth... gone.
[excited] If you're enjoying the ride, hit subscribe, because the next four planets are giants, and the first one is fourteen times wider than the Moon.

[curious] Neptune. The farthest planet from the Sun, a deep blue ball of gas and ice, about fourteen times wider than the Moon.
Hold three fingers out at arm's length. Neptune would be wider than all three. A giant blue eye in the sky.
And it has the fastest winds in the solar system, around two thousand kilometers an hour. From Earth, you could watch its storms move.
Its pull on our oceans? Almost fourteen hundred times stronger than the Moon's. The seas wouldn't rise and fall. They'd be torn into a bulge that sweeps around the planet.
Neptune is blue and wild. The next planet is almost the same size, but it does something no other planet does. It rolls.

Uranus. Pale blue-green, and tipped over on its side. Its spin is tilted ninety-eight degrees, so it rolls around the Sun like a ball.
In our sky, its thin dark rings would stand up and down, like a giant target painted on the night.
Its pull on the oceans is more than a thousand times the Moon's, so Earth's seas would be wrecked just like with Neptune.
Uranus is strange. The next planet is beautiful, and it would fill almost half your sky.

[excited] Saturn. Thirty-three times wider than the Moon, and its rings stretch more than a quarter of a million kilometers across.
Put it where the Moon is, and the planet alone would be wider than your fist. The rings? Four fists wide. Almost half the sky.
And Saturn is so light for its size that it would float in a bathtub, if you could find a bathtub big enough.
[serious] But its pull is almost eight thousand times the Moon's. And here's the twist. Saturn is ninety-five times heavier than Earth. So Saturn wouldn't orbit us. We would orbit Saturn. Once every three days.
Saturn steals our sky. The last planet steals our planet.

[serious] Jupiter. The biggest planet of all. More than three hundred times heavier than Earth. Forty times wider than the Moon.
Put it where the Moon is, and it covers twenty degrees of sky. Two fists wide, with storms bigger than Earth swirling across it.
But Earth wouldn't just look at Jupiter. Earth would become one of its moons, circling it every day and a half. At that distance, we'd be closer to Jupiter than its moon Io, deep inside its deadly radiation belts.
And every orbit, Earth would slide into Jupiter's shadow. The Sun would go black for a couple of hours. Every. Single. Day and a half.
[mischievously] So you wouldn't have a moon anymore. You'd be living on one.

From a Moon you'd barely notice, to a planet that turns Earth into its moon, that's every planet in the Moon's place.
[excited] So which one would you want in your sky? Tell me in the comments. And if this blew your mind, hit like!
```
