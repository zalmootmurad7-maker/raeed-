**Description**
```
If you can jump half a meter on Earth, how high would that exact same jump take you on Mars, the Moon, Jupiter, Pluto, or a comet? And on which world does one normal jump mean you never come back down?

We take the same push-off (3.13 m/s) to 15 worlds, using real surface gravity and escape velocity data from NASA, and real missions: Apollo, Cassini-Huygens, Rosetta and the Philae lander.

TIMESTAMPS
00:00 The jump test: Earth, Mars, Moon, Jupiter
00:53 Jupiter: 20 centimeters
01:33 Neptune, Uranus, Saturn & Venus
02:41 Mars & Mercury: the same jump
03:51 The Moon
04:34 Titan: jump, then fly
05:14 Eris & Pluto
05:43 Phobos: higher than the Burj Khalifa
06:34 Deimos: become a moon of Mars
07:19 Comet 67P: you never come down

THE NUMBERS (same 3.13 m/s jump everywhere)
Earth 0.50 m · Jupiter 0.20 m · Neptune 0.44 m · Uranus 0.56 m · Saturn/Venus 0.55 m
Mars & Mercury 1.32 m · Moon 3.02 m · Titan 3.63 m · Eris 6.0 m · Pluto 7.9 m (10 s in the air)
Phobos ≈ 900 m (≈ 20 min) · Deimos ≈ 2.9 km (over an hour) · escape velocity on Deimos 5.6 m/s
Comet 67P: escape velocity ≈ 1 m/s, so one normal jump leaves forever
Philae (2014) bounced off 67P at ~0.38 m/s and drifted for almost 2 hours
```
