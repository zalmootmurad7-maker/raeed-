# Episode 6 — What If We Built a Bridge Across the Pacific?

**Identity check:** one impossible scenario (drive from Japan to California on a bridge) + the real engineering/physics of what breaks.
**Formula:** Hook 0–5 s → Setup 5–20 s → Science 20–90 s → Consequences 90–180 s → Twist + question to the next video.
**Target length:** ~2:45 after `tighten.py`. ElevenLabs v3, voice "Mark", Speed 1.1. No fictional viewer ("Owen" removed) — the viewer is "you".

## Voice script (paste into ElevenLabs as one file)

```
[excited] To drive from Japan to California, you'd need a bridge three thousand times longer than the longest floating bridge ever built.

[mischievously] So let's build it anyway. Tokyo to San Francisco. Eight thousand kilometers of road. You, in your car, eighty hours of driving. No flights, ever again. And before this video is over, I'll show you the thing that actually breaks this bridge. Spoiler: it's not the ocean.

[curious] Step one. Hold up the road. Normal bridges stand on pillars. So how deep is the water?
The Pacific is about four kilometers deep on average. Its deepest trench goes down almost eleven. Flip Mount Everest upside down, drop it in, and it still doesn't touch the bottom.
The deepest bridge pillar ever built stands in about sixty-five meters of water. The deepest fixed oil platform, about five hundred. We'd need towers eight times taller than that. Thousands of them.
[excited] So, forget pillars. We float the bridge!
Floating bridges are real. The longest one, near Seattle, is two point three kilometers long. It sits on giant concrete pontoons, chained to the lake bed.
Ours needs to be three thousand five hundred times longer. And chained to the bottom of the Pacific. Four kilometers down. Nobody has ever anchored anything that deep.

[serious] Now the ocean fights back.
This isn't a lake. The Pacific makes typhoons. Waves taller than a six-story building, slamming into your road, all day, every day, for decades. One weak spot, and the whole thing snaps.
[mischievously] Fine. Let's cheat. Build artificial islands every four kilometers, and connect them with short bridges.
That's two thousand islands. Each one a tiny offshore city, floating in open ocean, with no land in sight.
[curious] But wait. Ships. Every year, thousands of giant cargo ships cross the Pacific. Your bridge just built an eight thousand kilometer wall across their path. So every island needs a drawbridge. Two thousand drawbridges.
[excited] And then… the bill.
The longest sea bridge in the world, in China, cost about three hundred million dollars per kilometer. And that's in shallow water, next to the coast.
Times eight thousand kilometers? Almost three trillion dollars. And the deep ocean costs far more.

[mischievously] So here's the twist. The depth? You can float around it. The storms? You can engineer around them. The ships? Drawbridges.
But two thousand floating cities that all have to survive decades of typhoons without a single failure? That's what breaks.
Not the physics. The bill.
[curious] And if you're thinking, just dig a tunnel? It's worse. Four hundred times the pressure of the air you're breathing, under the seafloor, for eight thousand kilometers. The longest undersea tunnel on Earth is thirty-eight kilometers.
[excited] So, bridge or tunnel? Comment BRIDGE or TUNNEL, and tell me what we should build next!
If this blew your mind, hit like!
```
