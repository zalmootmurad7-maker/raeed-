# Format 3 scripts (for ElevenLabs v3, voice "Mark", calm narrator: [curious] / [calm], Speed 1.0)

## 1. Twin paradox — "Two Twins, One Flies to a Star. Who Comes Back Older?"  (~45 s, 118 words)
```
[curious] Two twins. Same birthday. One stays on Earth. The other flies to a star five light-years away, at ninety-nine percent of the speed of light, then turns around and comes home.
On Earth, a little over ten years go by. Your twin waits, gets older, and finally watches the ship land.
But the twin who flew? For them, the whole trip took about seventeen months.
[calm] Same birthday. Now one is ten years older than the other. Nobody cheated. Time itself ran slower for the one who moved.
And it isn't just a thought experiment. NASA had real twins. Scott Kelly spent a year in orbit, and came back a few milliseconds younger than his brother Mark.
So... which twin would you rather be?
```
Numbers: γ at 0.99c = 7.09; 10 ly round trip → Earth 10.1 yr, traveller 10.1/7.09 = 1.42 yr ≈ 17 months.
Scott Kelly (340 days on the ISS, 2015–16): a few milliseconds younger than Mark (NASA Twins Study; reported ~5 ms).

## 2. Teleporter to Mars — "A Machine Sends You to Mars. Did You Travel, or Die?"  (~48 s, 125 words)
```
[curious] You step into a machine on Earth. It scans every atom in your body, sends the blueprint to Mars at the speed of light, and builds you there. Perfectly. Same memories, same scars, same you.
The trip takes about twenty minutes. Then the machine on Earth erases the original.
The you on Mars opens their eyes. Feels fine. Remembers stepping in on Earth.
[calm] So... did you travel to Mars? Or did you die on Earth, while someone who thinks they're you woke up?
Here's the strange part. Physics already does this. In nineteen ninety-seven, scientists teleported the state of a particle of light. And it only works one way: the original is always destroyed. Nature does not allow perfect copies.
So... would you step in?
```
Numbers: Earth–Mars light time 3–22 min (we say "about twenty minutes" for a far-side trip; ok as "about").
1997 quantum teleportation (Bouwmeester et al., Innsbruck; Boschi et al., Rome); no-cloning theorem: the original state is destroyed.

## 3. Rope around Earth — "A Rope Around Earth, Plus One Meter. Can Your Cat Fit Under?"  (~30 s, 82 words)
```
[curious] Wrap a rope tight around Earth's equator. Forty thousand kilometers. Now add just one meter, and lift it evenly, all the way around the planet.
How high does it rise? Enough for an ant? Your cat? You?
[calm] About sixteen centimeters. Everywhere. Your cat could walk under it, around the whole Earth.
And here's the weird part. It doesn't matter how big the ball is. A tennis ball, Earth, or the Sun. One extra meter always lifts it sixteen centimeters.
Did you guess right?
```
Numbers: equator 40,075 km; Δr = 1 m / 2π = 15.9 cm, independent of radius.
